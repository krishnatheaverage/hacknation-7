# Rental Housing Law Navigator: Method Note (one page)

**Team Pin Cite:** Gulnur Bekmukhanbetova, lead and legal architect: defined the problem and the acceptance criteria (the five tests as legal outcomes, the per-state precedence rules the engine applies, the "unknown, not a guess" rules for missing facts, and a gold set of 11 addresses with the correct answer in every category); reviewed and corrected the rule cards, effective dates and citations against official sources; resolved the legal city for the 500 addresses; ran the final check of the submission against the brief.
Krishna Harish, engineering: built the extraction pipeline, rules engine, geocoding and app to that specification. · **Demo:** https://hacknation-7.vercel.app · **Repo:** https://github.com/krishnatheaverage/hacknation-7 · **As-of date:** 2026-10-01 · Not legal advice.

**What it answers.** For any of the 500 sample addresses (or a typed US address) and a date: which rules in the six categories apply, which are unknown, superseded, not yet effective or pending, and how the five supplied change cases move the answer. Every line cites a source document, its retrieval date and a verbatim quoted span.

**Module A: Extract (automated).** Claude reads each of the 54 corpus texts plus 15 official pages we added for link-only sources (`corpus_extra/`, one page per law, never crawled) and writes rule records in the organizers' schema (`scripts/extract.ts`, cached per document in `out/audit/extract/`). Every quoted span is checked word for word against the source; a second pass (`scripts/verify.ts`) must quote support for each field and rejects cards whose rule is not in the source. Records of the same law from several documents are merged into one card (`scripts/group.ts`). A cite-check (`scripts/citecheck.ts`) then writes each citation as code + section and adds a passage from the law's own text (statutes and codes saved in `corpus_crosscheck/`), checking every number in the citation and every word of the passage against those texts. Result: 71 rule cards: 64 in force, 1 enacted but not yet effective (NJ FAIR Act, 2027-07-01), 2 pending (MA H.5222, S.2983), 4 failed (MA ballot question struck 2026-06-23; Boston H.3744, two cards; Hoboken 2024 referendum).

**Module B: Resolve and apply.** The Census Geocoder returns the incorporated place and county for each address (results cached in `data/derived/`), so Dorchester → Boston, Roxbury → Boston, San Ysidro → San Diego. Building facts come from the assessor rows; unit counts are read from land-use codes where the row has none ("3S-F-D-6U" = 6 units; New Jersey property class 4C = 5 or more families under N.J.A.C. 18:12-2.2). A deterministic engine (`src/lib/law/engine.ts`, no model at query time) tests each rule's coverage predicates and date. Missing facts produce **unknown** with the missing fact named: no year built, a building finished in a cutoff year (certificate of occupancy ≠ year built), owner type or owner occupancy, the NJ 30-year new-construction exemption (depends on a filing we cannot see). Precedence is declared per state by counsel: MA c. 40P § 4 preempts local rent control; a stricter CA local ordinance supersedes the statewide cap and just-cause rule; the NJ FAIR Act § 6(b) is flagged as a possible conflict with the Jersey City and Hoboken ordinances once in force.

**Module C: Change cases.** For each test the engine runs at both dates and lists addresses whose answer changes: T1 250 CA addresses (AB 325, not yet effective 2025-12-31 → applies 2026-01-02); T2 Hoboken ban at 40 Hoboken addresses, Jersey City ban at 50 Jersey City addresses, none in Newark; T3 140 NJ addresses (FAIR Act not yet effective → applies 2027-07-02), 90 flagged for preemption review; T4 110 MA addresses would be affected if the two bills passed, reported as pending; T5 empty: no rent cap is ever reported in Boston or Cambridge.

**Responsible design.** Every interface says "not legal advice". Enacted, pending, not-yet-effective and failed are kept apart. Each answer carries the as-of date, the retrieval date of its sources, a confidence level with reasons, and a conflict flag for a person to review. `out/audit/` holds the model output per document, the verification log and our self-check, so another person can reproduce every result with `npm run pipeline`. We use public data only and no customer, resident or pricing data.

**Known limits.** Nine cities have addresses (Santa Ana rules extracted, no addresses). Unit counts read from codes are ranges, so thresholds inside the range stay unknown. Year built stands in for the certificate date except in cutoff years. Our self-check (`scripts/selfscore.py`) is our own estimate, not the organizers' key. Quotes from the 15 pages we added do not count toward the citation score and are marked "added by the team". Open questions we surface: Berkeley ch. 13.63 has two published effective dates (2026-03-01 in Ordinance 7,974-N.S. vs "January 2026" in a law-firm alert; Ordinance 7,992-N.S. later rewrote the chapter without the March 1 clause); LA's new RSO formula (2026-02-02 per LAHD vs 2026-01-24 per a landlord association; the official code gives Ord. No. 188,795, effective 2026-02-02); California's 2026 screening-fee figure has no single official source; the FAIR Act may preempt the two NJ local bans.
